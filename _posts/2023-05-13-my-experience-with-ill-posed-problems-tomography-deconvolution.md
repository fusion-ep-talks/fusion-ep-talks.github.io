### Abstract
🖥️ Presenter for the FusionEPtalks #81 is Dr. Jan Mlynář, born in Prague 56 years ago, his keen interest in physics started already at the end of elementary school, and learned in some detail about fusion research in the eighties. Passed his master level studies in nuclear physics at the Faculty of  mathematics and Physics, Charles University in Prague in 1991. Then finalized his Ph.D. in plasma physics at the same faculty in 1995. For five years he worked in Lausanne at tokamak TCV in his postdoc position. Then in 2003 he joined the team of Joint European torus in the UK where he spent 4 years. Since 2007 he returned to Prague and joined the COMPASS team and managed preparation of the fusion university programme in fusion at the Czech Technical University in Prague.

Conspectus: 
In tokamak data analyzes we are often dealing with ill-posed problems where sparse or erroneous data may seriously hamper the analysis result and produce artifacts. In my talk I shall briefly define ill-posedness, explain the cases of plasma tomography and spectral deconvolution and outline some basic ideas on how to treat the ill-posed problems using constraints. I will also explain the procedure that allowed me to consider the results of my analyses dependable and reliable contributions to tokamak data analyses.
